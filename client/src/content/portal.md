## Context


In 2013, Kobalt launched its first client Portal, offering direct access to music and royalty data at a time when transparency in publishing was rare. It redefined service standards across the industry.

As competitors caught up the legacy product, codebase and limitations of an on-prem stack limited what we could improve, and the cost of supporting clients began to grow again.

Fast forward to Autumn 2026, and the original portal has been retired. In its place is one platform serving four surfaces - Kobalt writers directly, publishers and their rosters, white-labelled partner portals, and most recently KOSIGN. 

---

## The Real Problem

The data was never the problem - accessing and deciphering meaningful value, however, always was. 

Royalties arrived as quarterly statements. Registrations were invisible until someone chased them. Sync opportunities surfaced at payment, if at all. New songs arrived by email, text, and even sticky note - often incomplete. And on top of that, sub-publishers had no way to assess their portfolio or offer their own writers a meaningful view.

Each of those gaps became a support request, so the cost of service scaled with the client base - just as the business needed to grow without it. Larger catalogues, multi-currency accounting and demand for partner portals added to the pressure.

The challenge was to serve very different users - an individual songwriter, a publisher managing a roster, a partner operating under its own brand - without building and maintaining separate products.

## My Role

I led experience strategy and execution across the platform, partnering with product, engineering, A&R, copyright, and client service teams to design and deliver the new experience before migrating clients away from the legacy product.

That spanned the core journeys - royalty reporting, catalogue, registrations, sync and song submissions - and the foundations that let it flex across surfaces: a shared design system, a permissions model, B2B capabilities, and the accompanying white-label configuration.

It’s also these same foundations that underpin KOSIGN, the self-serve surface, which operates as a proving ground for experiments brought back to Kobalt clients.

## Reflection


The Portal reinforced that better tooling isn't only about information architecture or interaction. Adoption depended on performance, trust, and deliberate choices about which tasks to hand back to clients - and how - so that moving away from a tool built for super-users didn't create more work for client service teams.

The biggest lesson was about habits. When usage is cyclical, the product has to earn its place between cycles - and more usage isn't always good news. A client logging in to chase a missing payment is a failure, not engagement. We stopped treating DAU/MAU as the goal and designed for confidence instead: answers available when needed, and tasks like song submission that feel quick and worth doing. The real bar is reduction in time spent supporting and servicing client issues that should really be designed away.
